Research · Mechanics, models and computation

From a physical question
to a numerical model.

My research brings continuum mechanics, microstructural modelling and scientific computing together to understand the behaviour of soft biological and biomimetic materials.

01 / THE MATERIAL LAW

Constitutive modelling
of soft materials.

How should a model describe a material that is nonlinear, direction-dependent and sensitive to its loading history? I work on finite element-based material models for soft tissues and biomimetic hydrogel microstructures, with particular interests in hyperelasticity and viscoelasticity.

The aim is to connect a clear mathematical description of material behaviour with a numerical implementation that can be calibrated and assessed against experimental data.

  • Continuum mechanics
  • Hyperelasticity
  • Viscoelasticity
  • Parameter identification

02 / THE ROLE OF STRUCTURE

Microstructure &
multiscale mechanics.

Internal architecture influences a material’s overall response. I use representative volume element (RVE) models to study fibre-reinforced biological and biomimetic materials, connecting local deformation with effective material behaviour.

This work focuses on how fibre orientation, reinforcement and matrix response interact under mechanical loading.

  • Representative volume elements
  • Fibre-reinforced materials
  • Anisotropy
  • Homogenisation

03 / THE NUMERICAL METHOD

Computation as
a research instrument.

Scientific programming is an integral part of my work: implementing models, preparing finite element analyses, identifying material parameters and interpreting results.

My published research also spans musculoskeletal biomechanics, implant materials and thermal analysis. Across these applications, the central task is to translate a physical question into a tractable computational model.

  • Finite element analysis
  • Numerical implementation
  • Inverse modelling
  • Data analysis

From formulation to interpretation

A connected way of working.

01 / DEFINE

The question.

Identify the physical system, the quantities of interest and the evidence a model needs to explain.

02 / FORMULATE

The mechanics.

Set out the kinematics, constitutive assumptions and boundary conditions explicitly.

03 / IMPLEMENT

The computation.

Develop numerical workflows, check their behaviour and examine the influence of modelling choices.

04 / ASSESS

The evidence.

Identify parameters, compare with available data and interpret results within the model’s scope.
